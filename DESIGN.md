# Torrent TV design system

## Direction

**Night Screen.** The picture is the show; the interface steps back. A cool near-black ground, white type in one quiet variable face (Inter), translucent white pills instead of outlined boxes, and a single saffron accent kept for the few things that must stand out: progress, Best match, the selected marker, counts and badges. Depth comes from tonal fills and the backdrop scrims, not from hairlines or glass. It is not a trademark clone of any streaming product. Static artwork, typography and selection state carry the experience; autoplay video backgrounds are intentionally excluded.

Night Screen replaces "Velvet Booth" (ticket #29, aubergine-black with saffron as the primary-action fill and Bricolage Grotesque). Decisions recorded in spec #51 and its tickets (the approved 1920x1080 mockup boards: Home, Navigation open, Movie title page, Series page, Search, Downloads, Settings, Player controls); the earlier prototypes are kept in `docs/design/direction-prototypes.html`:

- Ground: neutral, slightly cool near-blacks (`#0a0b0f` to `#262a35`), so poster art supplies the colour.
- Accent: refined saffron, `#f2a93b`, for progress, Best match, the selected-tab marker, counts and badges. Never a large button fill and never a focus colour.
- Focus: white. A focused button, chip, tab or rail item turns solid white with ground-coloured text, inside a white ring; a focused row or poster keeps its own fill, lighter, inside the ring (built with #53, see Focus).
- Font: one bundled variable family, Inter (SIL OFL 1.1, bundled offline, Latin plus Latin Extended for Romanian diacritics, about 87 KB). Headings 700 to 800 with slight negative tracking, body 400 to 500, tabular figures for numbers.
- Artwork: a single blurred poster layer behind rows, following the focused title, with a scrim that guarantees text contrast. On a TV the layer is the half-resolution art scaled up with no blur filter and no crossfade: measured on a 2023 Samsung (Tizen 9), the blur and the per-key motion stalled the main thread 300-1000 ms per frame, so every TV takes the lean path and snaps. Posters themselves stay sharp. Titles without artwork get a designed tinted backdrop, never an empty hole.
- One visual language for the browser app and the TV client. Both import the same token file. Density differs by platform through the type scale and target sizes, never through a second palette.
- Dark only. There is no light theme.
- Signature: the backdrop follows focus. Beside it, source facts (resolution, codec, size, seeders) sit in a spec strip set apart from titles.

Hard limits that hold for every screen: an original look, legibility at 3 m on the TV, an obvious focus state that does not depend on one CSS feature, no looping decorative animation, contrast at WCAG AA, and CSS that stays inside the support floor below.

## Tokens

The single source is `clients/shared/design/tokens.css` (custom properties, all prefixed `--tt-`) and `clients/shared/design/fonts.css` (the bundled face). Entry points import them as `@torrent-tv/shared/design/fonts.css` and `@torrent-tv/shared/design/tokens.css`: `clients/tv/src/main.tsx` (so the Tizen, webOS and Android builds get them), `web/src.tsx`, and `desktop/src/App.tsx`. Screens read tokens with `var(--tt-...)` and never copy a hex value. `clients/shared/src/design-tokens.test.ts` checks the file: required names, contrast, the forbidden-CSS list, motion limits and the font rules.

The legacy variables (`--teal`, `--panel`, `--ink` and the rest) are gone from the TV client: ticket #30 rewrote `clients/tv/src/tv.css` and `performance.css` on the `--tt-` tokens, and `clients/tv/src/design-tokens.test.ts` fails if a legacy variable, a colour of its own (other than video black and subtitle white) or a literal TV font size under 26 px comes back.

The browser app (`web/style.css`) and the desktop shell (`desktop/src/shell.css`) are on the tokens too (ticket #31) and define no legacy variable: every value is a `--tt-` token, plus a few layout locals prefixed `--w-` (rail widths, gutter, text measure). `web/style-contract.test.ts` reads both sheets and fails on a `--teal`-style variable, a copied hex color, a looping animation, a transition outside opacity, color, transform and shadow, text under 13 px, a removed outline, a missing reduced-motion or forced-colors block, and a backdrop scrim that would drop muted text below 4.5:1 over an all-white poster.

The translucent fills are checked twice: `--tt-text` and `--tt-text-muted` hold 4.5:1 on either fill over each surface, and on either fill over the title-page text scrim (`--tt-scrim-text`) over an all-white picture (8.6:1 and 5.8:1 on the rest fill, 7.6:1 and 5.2:1 on the selected fill). `--tt-text-subtle` falls to 3.7:1 in that over-art case, which is why it is for the plain ground only. The white focus ring holds 9.2:1 or more against a selected fill on every surface.

Rules. Saffron marks progress and a few chosen things (Best match, the selected tab, counts, badges); it is never a large button fill, never focus, and never the only signal of a state. Status colors always travel with an icon or a word (`Downloaded`, `Needs attention`). Because warn sits close to saffron in hue, warn text uses `--tt-warn` on a surface and never on a saffron fill. Translucent colors are written `rgba()`; the TV engines reject 4- and 8-digit hex.

### Type

One family, Inter. `--tt-font-sans` is `"Inter", system-ui, "Segoe UI", Arial, "Helvetica Neue", sans-serif`. Weights: `--tt-weight-regular` 400 and `--tt-weight-medium` 500 for body, `--tt-weight-semibold` 600 for labels and buttons, `--tt-weight-bold` 700 for row and section headings, `--tt-weight-black` 800 for titles and the display size. Headings take slight negative tracking: `--tt-tracking-display` -0.025em on titles and the display size, `--tt-tracking-heading` -0.02em on page headings, `--tt-tracking-snug` -0.01em on row headings. Line height `--tt-leading-display` 1.02 for titles, `--tt-leading-tight` 1.15 for headings, `--tt-leading-body` 1.45 for body. Numbers (times, sizes, counts, speeds) use tabular figures: `font-variant-numeric: var(--tt-figures)` (`tabular-nums`, the font's `tnum` feature is kept in the subset).

| Role | Web (`--tt-text-*`) | TV at 1920x1080 (`--tt-tv-text-*`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| meta, captions, badges, chips | xs 13 px | meta 26 px |
| secondary and body | sm 15 px, md 16 px | body 30 px |
| lead, row headings | lg 20 px | lead 34 px |
| page heading (Downloads, Settings) | xl 26 px | heading 56 px |
| title on its page | 2xl 36 px | title 88 px (movie), title-compact 80 px (series, longer names) |
| hero | display 52 px | display 92 px |

The web scale is unchanged by #52 and kept by the browser alignment (#63): it is already the dense end of the same ramp, and 13 px stays the browser floor. Browser buttons and labels take semibold 600 like the TV's. On the TV nothing a viewer must read goes below 26 px: `clients/shared/src/design-tokens.test.ts` keeps every `--tt-tv-text-*` token at 26 px or more and in rising order, and `clients/tv/src/design-tokens.test.ts` rejects a literal font size under 26 px in `tv.css`. Numbers in source facts keep weight 500 or more.

Font loading. The bundled files are `clients/shared/design/fonts/inter-latin-wght.woff2` (32.8 KB) and `inter-latin-ext-wght.woff2` (56.1 KB), built from the official Inter 4.1 release by `tools/subset_inter.py`: weight axis 400 to 800, optical size pinned to 14 (the text design, which holds up best at 3 m), split by unicode-range so the Latin Extended file (Romanian ă ș ț, both comma and cedilla forms) loads only when such a letter appears. Provenance, hashes and the licence are in `clients/shared/design/fonts/README.md` and `OFL.txt`. `fonts.css` declares the face only inside `@supports (font-variation-settings: normal)` (Chromium 62+). webOS 4 runs Chromium 53, which cannot apply a weight axis and would render the file's default instance (Regular) at every weight, so it keeps its platform sans. Tizen 5 (Chromium 63), Android TV and every browser get the real face. Both TV startup pages set their `html, body` font from the token too, because an inline `font-family` there would otherwise win over `:root`, and their `var()` fallbacks repeat the token colours (the TV token test checks they match). `font-display: swap` keeps text visible while the local file loads. The TV builds inline the font into `app.css` as a data URI (Vite library mode, covered by the page `data:` source in the CSP), which makes `app.css` about 187 KB; the browser build emits two hashed `.woff2` files. The shared token test reads each woff2's character map and fails if a Romanian letter or basic Latin character is not drawn by the file whose unicode-range claims it.

### Spacing, shape, targets

- Spacing is a 4 px scale: `--tt-space-1` to `--tt-space-9` = 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96. Layouts use margins, not `gap`, on the TV engines (ADR-0006).
- TV safe area is 5% of the canvas: `--tt-tv-safe-x` 96 px, `--tt-tv-safe-y` 54 px. Text and focus rings stay inside it; backdrops and the scrim bleed to the edge. The canvas is fixed at 1920x1080 and scales for 4K output.
- Radii: `--tt-radius-sm` 6, `--tt-radius-md` 10, `--tt-radius-lg` 16 (cards, dialogs), `--tt-radius-xl` 20 (large cards and rows), `--tt-radius-pill` (buttons and chips are fully rounded in this direction; screens move to pills as they are restyled). Depth comes from tonal separation and the backdrop, not from glass or `backdrop-filter`.
- Controls (TV, #53, `clients/tv/src/tv.css` "Controls"): every button and chip is a fully rounded pill on `--tt-fill` with no hairline; the border stays as a transparent 2 px edge so focus never changes geometry. The primary action is the brighter `--tt-fill-strong` pill with the bold word (saffron is no longer a button fill). Selected tabs, filters and toggles (`.active`, `.open`, `aria-pressed`) take `--tt-fill-selected`, the bold word and a short saffron marker (40 x 4 px) under the label; focused, the pill turns white and keeps the marker in `--tt-accent-deep`. Rows (version rows, episode and Info rows, discovered servers, Player panel rows) and cards (posters, category tiles, project links, the promotion slot) use the card radii (`--tt-radius-lg`/`--tt-radius-xl`), not pills. Non-interactive chips (version quality chips, tracker readiness) are pills on the fill with no border; a status chip keeps its word beside its colour. Inputs stay fields: raised fill and the `--tt-line-strong` 3:1 edge.
- Destructive actions are distinct but calm: the word is in `--tt-danger` on the ordinary pill (no red border box); focused, the pill fills with `--tt-danger` and the word turns `--tt-on-fill-focus` (ground colour, about 9:1), so a destructive control under the cursor never looks like the white focus of a safe one. Confirmation dialogs still put Cancel first.
- Icons (TV): one module, `clients/tv/src/icons.tsx`, of inline SVG line icons on a 24 px grid with 2 px round-capped, round-joined strokes drawn in `currentColor` (solid shapes only for play, pause, the rating star and the "more" dots). It replaces every Unicode glyph the client used (rail glyphs, the ← on back buttons, the › row chevrons, the ★ before a rating) and absorbs the Player's first icon set under the same names. Icons are decorative (`aria-hidden`); the control always has a visible label or an aria-label. Sizes: 36 px inside the Player's round controls (44 px on Play/Pause), 32 px in the rail and in front of a button label, 24 px inline in a card's meta line. The breadcrumb separator (›) and the middle dot between facts are typography and stay as characters.
- App mark: a 56 px rounded square (`--tt-radius-lg`) in `--tt-accent` with a play shape in `--tt-on-accent` (`AppMark` in icons.tsx), at the top of the rail, on the startup page (96 px) and on the Setup screen (72 px), replacing the "TT" letter block. The injected app name (`app-name.ts`) still names the app; the monogram is no longer drawn.
- Targets: `--tt-target-web` 44 px minimum, `--tt-tv-target` 72 px for TV buttons and rows. The TV rail rows are 76 px (#54: six hubs fit 1080 px, so the 56 px exception for fifteen destinations is gone).

### Focus

Focus is two independent signals so it survives an engine that drops one declaration: a solid white outline (`--tt-focus-ring`, 4 px on TV via `--tt-focus-width-tv`, 3 px in the browser via `--tt-focus-width-web`) drawn outside an offset gap (`--tt-focus-offset`), plus a white inner edge (`--tt-focus-edge`) on the element's own border. On the TV add a scale of `--tt-focus-scale-tv` and the soft white halo `--tt-focus-shadow-tv` (`0 0 0 5px` white at 25%, then a drop shadow, as on the mockups). The ring sits outside the element, so it contrasts with the surrounding surface (at least 14.3:1) and stays visible on any fill. The ring went from saffron-light to white with #52 (the accent is no longer a focus colour). The second signal depends on what has focus (#53): a button, chip, tab or rail item turns solid white (`--tt-fill-focus`) with ground-coloured text (`--tt-on-fill-focus`); a row or poster keeps its own look and takes a lighter fill (`--tt-fill-selected` on rows, `--tt-overlay` under a poster's copy) inside the ring, so its muted lines and saffron badges keep their contrast; a destructive button fills with `--tt-danger`. On old engines the outline is drawn as a rectangle around a pill (rounded outlines arrived in Chromium 94); the white fill and the halo still follow the radius. The browser uses `:focus-visible`; the TV stylesheet uses `:focus` because Chromium 63 and 53 lack `:focus-visible`, and TV focus is never pointer-driven. Neighbouring posters in a focused row may dim to about 70% opacity; the focused one never does. `tools/webos_ipk.py` requires the solid outline beside any `button:focus` box-shadow.

As built on the TV: poster cards and landscape category tiles (both `.poster-card`) and the primary action of the Home hero and of the title page lift under focus with `scale(var(--tt-focus-scale-tv))`. On capable engines the scale eases over `--tt-dur-fast` on `transform` alone, a compositor property; on lean engines (every TV browser — Tizen, webOS, Android TV WebView — and any Chromium before 70, which `clients/tv/src/capability.ts` marks with the `lean-graphics` class on the root) it applies at once, because the transition cost a layer-tree rebuild per frame there and stretched every D-pad move's settle time (ticket #49). The backdrop layer carries no `will-change`, which doubled paint cost per move. Every other control keeps its geometry (rows and tabs change only fill and colour on focus, which `focus-ring.test.ts` checks), so lists never shift while the remote moves, and `performance.css` pins `transform: none` for them. The 70% dim is not built, because it needs `:focus-within` (Chromium 60+). A ring is clipped by an `overflow` container, so rails, grids and scrolling lists reserve at least 16 px of padding around their focusable children; a control inside a clipped card (a season pack header) lives outside the card's `overflow` instead.

### Motion

Transitions only, on opacity, color, transform and shadow: `--tt-dur-fast` 120 ms (focus, press), `--tt-dur-base` 180 ms (menus, rails), `--tt-dur-slow` 260 ms (backdrop crossfade, sidebar), easing `--tt-ease-out`. Nothing loops; no `infinite` and no `@keyframes` in the shared sheets. Never animate `width`, `height`, `top`, `left` or a blur radius. The browser honors `prefers-reduced-motion` by dropping transitions to zero; the TV has no such setting and keeps the short ones.

### Backdrop art

- One blurred layer per screen, behind the rows, sourced from the focused or featured title's backdrop (falling back to its poster). Never one blur per card.
- The blur is static. Change the layer by crossfading opacity over `--tt-dur-slow`; never animate the blur. Browser blur radius is `--tt-backdrop-blur-web` (36 px). On the TV render the layer at half resolution and scale it up with `transform`, with the smaller radius `--tt-backdrop-blur-tv` (18 px); Tizen and webOS GPUs choke on a full-resolution blur. The layer is never larger than the viewport, and the TV never uses `backdrop-filter`.
- Artwork opacity is `--tt-backdrop-art-opacity` (0.55). A scrim sits over it: `--tt-scrim-top` to `--tt-scrim-bottom` vertically, and `--tt-scrim-text` (84% ink) directly under any text block. That keeps body text at 4.5:1 or better over an all-white poster.
- No artwork: the backdrop becomes `--tt-ink` with a soft radial of `--tt-accent-soft`, and the poster fallback shows the title's initial on `--tt-raised`. That state is designed, not an error.
- TV as built (`clients/tv/src/backdrop.tsx`): two stacked layers inside the viewport, 960x540 and scaled by 2, blurred at `--tt-backdrop-blur-tv`, only one on at a time; a change preloads the image, then flips the `on` class, so the cost is one decode and one opacity fade. A card or button opts in with `data-art="<url>"` (empty means no artwork and brings the fallback back); a control without `data-art` leaves the art alone; the art follows focus after 180 ms, so a held D-pad key loads only where it stops. Home and the list screens start from the featured or first title, the detail screen holds its own title's art (`follow={false}`, drawn sharp, see below), and Setup shows the fallback only. Text blocks that sit on art (the hero, the detail header) carry their own `--tt-scrim-text` panel.
- Title page art is the exception to the blur: opening a title shows its own picture as it is. The TV detail screen passes `sharp` to the backdrop: one full-resolution 1920x1080 layer, no blur, no scale, at full opacity (not `--tt-backdrop-art-opacity`), so there is no filter pass for an old GPU. The TMDB backdrop is used; a poster stands in only when there is no backdrop and is placed full height at the right edge (`poster-only`) instead of being cropped into a stretched slice. Legibility comes from the scrim, not the blur, and there is no panel under the title block (#56, the board's "no boxed header"): from the bottom edge `--tt-scrim-bottom` through `--tt-scrim-text` at 32% to clear at 58% (under the versions), and from the left edge `--tt-scrim-bottom` through `--tt-scrim-text` at 38% to clear at 72% (under the copy), so the upper right of the picture stays clear. The browser's title overlay does the same: an undimmed, unblurred backdrop under a left scrim that fades to transparent. Not verified on hardware: the legibility of the muted facts and overview straight on a bright picture at 3 m, and the poster-only placement on a real set.
- No autoplay video backdrops.

## Web and desktop layout

The browser app and the desktop shell are pointer and keyboard first, so they use the dense end of the shared scale (15 px secondary text, 44 px minimum targets) and the same palette as the TV, never a second one. Dark only.

- Rail (#64). The rail holds the TV's six hubs, in its order and with its icons: Home, Search, Library, Discover, Downloads, Settings, one list with no group labels. `web/hubs.ts` groups the views exactly as `clients/tv/src/hubs.ts` groups the TV routes (`web/hubs.test.tsx` fails if they drift); it is presentation over the views, so every URL is unchanged (`/library/dashboard`, `/library/continue`, `/library/favorites`, `/library/watched`, `/library/categories` are Library tabs; `/tracker/dashboard` (Highlights), `/tracker/browse`, `/tracker/categories` are Discover tabs; `/library/downloads` is Downloads; `/jobs`, `/jobs/<id>` and `/projects` belong to Settings) and a refresh or a shared link lands on the same hub and tab. The current hub (the one holding the view on screen, so Settings on Background jobs) sits on `--tt-fill-selected` with the bold word and a short saffron bar, hover is one fill step, keyboard focus is the white item (#63). A Library or Discover item opens the hub on its last-used tab (kept in `localStorage` as `web.hubTabs`, like the TV's), else the first. At 1100 px and wider the rail is 232 px with labels. From 641 to 1099 px it narrows to 72 px of icons, each with a `title` and a visually hidden label so every item keeps its name. At 640 px and below it becomes a bottom bar with the label under each icon and the selected item marked by a bar on top; the six items share the width (each its label's width plus an equal share of the rest, the account button 64 px beside them when accounts are on), so they fit a 320 px phone without scrolling or clipping a label.
- Page heading and hub tabs (#64). Every page has one `h1`: the hub's name (Home, Search, Library, Discover, Downloads, Settings), or "Background jobs" and "Other projects" for the screens reached from Settings. On Library and Discover the tabs sit on the heading's line as pills (`nav.hub-tabs`, buttons with `aria-current="page"` on the selected one, which takes the shared selected look); a tab is a view with its own URL and pushes a history entry, so Back returns to the previous tab. On a phone the strip wraps under the heading and scrolls sideways on its own, the selected tab scrolled into sight. A single-shelf Library tab (Continue watching, My List, Watched) keeps its shelf heading for screen readers only, as the TV drops it. Settings ends its heading line with quiet pills for Background jobs, Other projects (only while the server publishes links; `/projects` still opens without them) and Report a bug (the issue tracker, in a new tab); Background jobs and Other projects start their heading line with a "Settings" back pill. The desktop shell keeps a 224 px rail and drops it to a row above the page under 720 px.
- Page. One column of content with a 32 px gutter (24 px on tablets, 16 px on phones), no horizontal scroll from 320 px up. Lists collapse to one column on phones; the title detail becomes a full-screen sheet.
- Home hero. A text panel on the `--tt-scrim-text` scrim beside a sharp 16:9 artwork tile; under 1100 px the columns narrow, on phones the tile moves above the text.
- Detail. A sharp poster beside the title block over the title's backdrop image (not blurred and not dimmed, under a left scrim that fades out), so the picture reads clearly and the page has no blurred layer. Season tabs follow, then a series shows its episode list, and OK on an episode swaps the list for that episode's full-width Versions view (see "Series page: episodes, then Versions" below); there is no per-episode artwork.
- Backdrop that follows focus. `web/backdrop.tsx` draws one fixed layer behind the page: two image slots that crossfade by opacity over `--tt-dur-slow`, blurred by `--tt-backdrop-blur-web`, under the `--tt-scrim-top` to `--tt-scrim-bottom` gradient. Poster and library cards publish their title's artwork on `pointerenter` and `focus`; the layer rests on the featured title (Home and catalog views) or the first library title (library views) and resets when the view changes. No artwork leaves the tinted radial of `--tt-accent-soft` on `--tt-ink`. Neighbouring cards in a rail dim to 72% while one is hovered or keyboard-focused.
- Version row. A spec strip: resolution with HDR, then quality and codec chips, size and seeders, and the action as a pill (`--tt-fill-strong`, white with ground-coloured text while the row is hovered or focused). The row is a quiet `--tt-fill-quiet` band with no hairline that takes `--tt-fill-selected` under the pointer or the keyboard; Best match stays the saffron badge. Wide rows (640 px and up, by container query) keep it on one line; narrow rows put the facts under the spec. The row is a button for the action plus a separate Details toggle; the toggle is never nested inside the action.
- Icons and app mark (#63). `web/icons.tsx` draws the TV's line icon set (`clients/tv/src/icons.tsx`) with the browser's own component: the same names and shapes on the 24 px grid, 2 px round strokes in `currentColor`, solid play, pause, star and "more" dots, plus a few browser-only icons by the same rules (alert, clock, refresh, user, bug, close, trash). Unknown names draw nothing. `web/icons.test.tsx` fails if a shared icon's shapes drift from the TV's. The rail uses the TV rail's icons item for item (and, since #64, its six hubs), and the brand shows `AppMark` (saffron rounded square with a play shape) instead of the "TT" block; the desktop shell takes the same mark. Back buttons and Info rows use the chevron icons instead of ← and ›.
- Controls (#63, the TV vocabulary from #53). Buttons are fully rounded pills on `--tt-fill` with no hairline (a transparent 1 px border keeps the geometry). ## TV interaction

**Declared D-pad maps are the source of truth.** Where every remote key moves focus, on every screen and overlay, is written down as plain data in `clients/tv/src/dpad/maps/` (one map per screen or overlay: the rail, Home, Search, each Library and Discover tab, category pages, Downloads, Background jobs and a job's detail, Settings and its dialogs, Other projects, Setup, the movie and series pages, an episode's Versions view, the Info panel and its Seasons levels, the Player controls and its Audio, Subtitles and Options panels; the format is described at the top of `clients/tv/src/dpad/model.mjs`). Each stop, addressed by its `data-focus-key` or as a member of a list (`posters[first]`, `[last]`, `[same]` for the same column, `[selected]`, `+1`, `+row`), declares Up, Down, Left and Right (and OK or Back where they matter): another stop, `stay`, `rail`, `return` (back to the overlay's opener) or a stop on another screen. `make dpad-tv` (CI runs it after the TV build) drives the built bundle in headless Chromium with real layout and the Tizen 5 user agent, presses every key from every list end and literal stop, replays held-key bursts at 100 ms along the long rows and lists, and fails on any move the map does not declare, focus lost to the page body or off screen, a stop left unrevealed, a landing on screen furniture, an unreachable stop, a trap, or Back not unwinding the top layer; the happy-dom tests (`dpad-maps.test.tsx`) read the same maps. Changing a screen's focus behaviour means changing its map in the same change; a redesign that moves stops edits the map first. The paragraphs below describe the maps in words.

The rail (#54, board "Navigation open") is compact until focus enters it. Left from content column zero (the first tab of a hub, the first card of a row, a field) opens it on the current hub; Up and Down walk the six hubs and stop at both ends; OK opens a hub (Library and Discover on their last-used tab, remembered across restarts, else their first); Right and Back restore the exact prior content control and collapse it. Compact, it is a 120 px strip of 64 px icon cells, the current hub's icon on `--tt-fill-selected`. Open, the same buttons (76 px rows, 448 px wide, 22 px radius, same positions, so no icon moves) show their labels (32 px semibold), the current hub on the selected fill, the focused one white with its one-line hint under the label in `--tt-text-faint` ("Continue · My List · Watched · Categories"; it may wrap to a second line); Downloads carries a saffron count of the transfers moving now; the foot says "Connected to <host>" beside a green dot (a warn-coloured dot and "Reconnecting…" while the event stream is down). As built, the rail is a 520 px element whose panel (a `::before` layer behind the buttons, the ground colour fading out over its last fifth) is slid left with a `transform` so only its 120 px fade shows behind the icons when compact, and slides home when open; the labels, count, brand name and connection line fade by `opacity`, and a full-screen veil (`--tt-veil`) fades in over the content. All of it is compositor-driven, so opening it never re-lays out or repaints the page and the main thread only flips a class; the only layout is the focused rail item growing for its hint, inside the rail's own containment. On lean engines (the `lean-graphics` root class) all of it snaps to the end state instead of animating; animating `clip` was the first attempt and held each route change open for the length of the transition on a TV-class CPU (ticket #49). Rows and columns are explicit, vertical movement clamps to the nearest available column, and Back unwinds detail/dialog/player layers before exiting.

Screen chrome (#54). No screen draws "Server connected" or a header Refresh button. A screen has one heading at 56 px (Library, Discover, Downloads, Settings, Background jobs, Other projects; none on Home and Search, where the hero and the field lead), and no second heading repeating it: Search results, Browse and the single-shelf Library tabs draw their rail without a title. On Library and Discover the tabs sit on the heading's line as pills, row 0 of the content focus graph: Left from the first tab opens the rail, Right stops at the last, Up stays, Down enters the content at its first stop (the first card of the first line, whatever tab it left), and Up from the content's top line returns to the selected tab (not the one in the same column). OK on a tab opens its route and the cursor lands on its first card; the selected tab is the `active` pill (selected fill, bold, short saffron bar). A banner (`.tv-banner`, warn bar on `--tt-fill`) appears above the content only while the server connection is lost or an enabled tracker is not set up. A manual reload is Reload library in Settings › Server and the next action of the empty catalog state. Background jobs keeps its screen; Back from it, and from Other projects, returns to its entry in Settings.

Landing focus. A screen opens with the cursor on its first card, never on a tab or other furniture (`clients/tv/src/landing.ts`). Screens tag what they draw with `data-landing`: `card` for a tile or row (poster, category tile, job, download, project link), `control` for a primary control that outranks the cards (the Home hero button, the Search field), `chrome` for screen furniture (a hub's tabs, the empty catalog's Reload library). The target is the tagged element with the lowest `data-focus-row`, then `data-focus-col`; a screen with no cards falls back to its first untagged content control (the first setting), and the chrome only when nothing else exists after a short grace period, the selected tab first. Cards that load late still receive the cursor, but a landing lets go as soon as the viewer presses a navigation key or moves focus, so it never yanks. The rule applies to menu route changes, closing a title page or dialog with no card to return to, a finished search (first result), and category pages; a screen that opens a sub-view itself calls `requestLanding()`.

Starting playback unmounts Catalog, so App keeps a `NavSnapshot` (route, open title and episode target, query, category, sort, the focused card key; `clients/tv/src/nav-snapshot.ts`) written only when the viewer starts a play or opens a download, never by a next-episode swap. When the Player closes, Catalog remounts with it as the optional `initialNav` prop (absent means the old behaviour: Home, nothing open): the route and filters return, an open title is fetched again through the normal open path (so Resume shows the new position) and focuses Play or Resume, and a list focuses the saved card when it still exists. If the title cannot be reopened the route list shows with the usual notice. The snapshot is dropped when the server changes or is forgotten.

Player keys follow the Wake model. On hidden controls the first directional press only wakes them and never seeks: Left or Right lands on the timeline (the next Left or Right seeks), Up or Down restores Control focus (the toolbar control last used, `play` by default). OK on hidden controls wakes them and activates that control, so Audio and Subtitles open on the first press. With a toolbar control focused, Left and Right move along the toolbar, and Up moves to the timeline. Seeking by one Seek step happens only with Timeline focus (Left and Right, focus stays on the timeline; Down returns to the remembered toolbar control) or with the fast-forward and rewind keys (and Next and Previous, a larger step; see Remote keys), which work in every state without moving focus and, with the controls hidden, show the position on the OSD without waking the controls. A seek records a pending target that owns the shown position and current time until AVPlay's `seekTo` succeeds, a playtime within 1 second of the target arrives, or 2 seconds pass; stale playtime is ignored meanwhile, and a seek error is shown as a status message and routed to recovery. Held keys: `event.repeat` is ignored for Enter, Back and Play/Pause, directional repeats keep moving focus, and timeline repeats keep adding Seek steps that commit after the last one. Focus stays on the pressed control after every action, including opening and closing a Player panel. The hide timer re-arms after every key press, a seek does not hold it open, and when it fires the focused control is remembered for the next Wake. If the active element is outside the control list, focus falls back to the remembered control, never the first. Dialogs remember and restore their launcher.

## Remote keys

One table for every remote, handled in the Player by `playerAction` and `PlayerKeyController` (`clients/tv/src/player.ts`, `player-keys.ts`). A key matches by `event.key` name or by `keyCode`, whichever the platform sends. Android TV delivers the key names natively (ticket #27); Tizen needs each name registered through `tizen.tvinputdevice.registerKey` (`registerMediaKeys` in `platform.ts`, one try/catch per key, because a name the firmware lacks throws); webOS delivers the keyCodes without registration.

| Action | Key names | keyCodes | In the Player |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Play | `MediaPlay` | 415 | Plays. Explicit, never toggles. |
| Pause | `MediaPause` | 19 | Pauses and saves the position. Explicit. |
| Play/Pause | `MediaPlayPause` | 10252 | Toggles. Ignored while held. |
| Stop | `MediaStop` | 413 | Saves and leaves the Player, like Back. |
| Rewind | `MediaRewind` | 412 | One Seek step back (10 s). |
| Fast-forward | `MediaFastForward` | 417 | One Seek step forward (10 s). |
| Previous | `MediaTrackPrevious` | 10232 | Skip step back (60 s). |
| Next | `MediaTrackNext` | 10233 | Skip step forward (60 s). |
| Previous episode | `ChannelDown` | 428 | Series: saves progress and starts the previous episode. Otherwise the OSD says "No previous episode". |
| Next episode | `ChannelUp` | 427 | Series: saves progress and starts the next episode. Otherwise the OSD says "No next episode". |
| Back | `GoBack`, `BrowserBack`, `XF86Back`, `Back` | 27, 10009 (Tizen), 461 (webOS) | Closes a Player panel, else leaves. |

Rules:

- Channel up and down are the episode skip (the episode queue): `POST /downloads/{id}/next-episode` and `/previous-episode` resolve the sibling from the series hierarchy. The Player saves the current progress first, drops any pending seek target, and swaps the player to the sibling; a result that arrives after the Player closed or suspended is discarded. A movie, or the first or last episode, gets a brief OSD notice instead. The Wake model is untouched: the keys never move Control focus, and held repeats are ignored. The media-track keys keep the plain seek: Next and Previous skip a larger step (`SKIP_STEP_MS`, 60 s) rather than change item. They use the same pending seek target, commit timer and clamping as Rewind and Fast-forward, so any mix of the four accumulates into one committed seek.
- Rewind, Fast-forward, Next and Previous work with the controls hidden or visible, repeat while held, and never move Control focus. Hidden, they show the new position on the OSD and leave the controls hidden (the Wake model above); visible, they re-arm the hide timer. Play, Pause and Play/Pause reveal the controls through the existing playback state change and do not move focus either.
- A media key typed while a text field has focus is ignored by the Player (`isEditableTarget`).
- Outside the Player the media keys are not mapped at all (`remoteAction` in `navigation.ts` returns null for them), so D-pad navigation, OK and Back are unaffected. Channel up and down are only the episode skip inside the Player.
- Tizen registers the eight media names plus `ChannelUp` and `ChannelDown`. The colour keys (`ColorF0Red` to `ColorF3Blue`) are deliberately not registered: nothing in the client uses them. The `tv.inputdevice` privilege is already in `clients/tizen/config.xml` and required by `tools/tizen_wgt.py`; the key names themselves are not listed in the config or the validator.
- Not verified on hardware: that each physical remote sends these codes (Channel up and down as episode skip is likewise unverified on hardware) (Samsung and LG remotes differ, and many have no Previous or Next button), that Tizen 5.0 firmware accepts every name, and that Channel up and down reach the page on both platforms. See the Remote keys rows in `docs/TIZEN.md` and `docs/WEBOS-VERIFICATION.md`.

## Best version

The primary Play on a title page prepares one Source chosen by `rankSources()` (`clients/shared/src/sources.ts`, unit-tested); the button names the choice (TV: "Play and download · 1080p WEB-DL", with the size on the line under the row; web: "Play and download · 1080p WEB-DL · 4.2 GB") and **Choose version** (web: the picker, whose default sort is Best match; TV: focus moves to the version list) shows every version in the same order. Resume still replaces Play. Ranking, in priority order:

1. Junk captures (CAM, TS, HDCAM, screeners, found in the quality or the release name) go last and lead only when nothing else exists.
2. Downloaded beats managed (queued, downloading, partial) beats a new Prepare.
3. A swarm of 3 or more seeders beats a thinner one; when none qualifies, seeders alone decide.
4. Resolution tier: 1080p, then 4K with HDR or 20+ seeders, 720p, plain 4K, lower, unknown. A small box progressing a 60 GB remux is a worse default than 1080p.
5. Quality: BluRay, REMUX or WEB-DL, then WEBRip, HDTV, unknown.
6. More seeders, then the smaller file. Ties keep the server order.

For a series the target is the resume episode, else the target episode of the opened link, else the first episode of the selected season; sources that name another season or episode rank after those that match. A season that only has packs shows no Play (the pack controls stay).

The TV title page opens with focus on Play or Resume (My List when there is nothing to play); Back stays one press Up and the Back key still closes the page. The season tab that opens first is the link's target, else the season of the saved progress, else the first.

## Support floor for shipped CSS

Everything shipped to the TV runs on Chromium 63 (Tizen 5) and Chromium 53 (webOS 4): no `gap`, `inset`, `aspect-ratio`, `:is()`, `:where()`, `:focus-visible`, `min()`, `max()`, `clamp()`; no 4- or 8-digit hex; grid only with a flex fallback (the TV stylesheet uses no grid at all, and keeps the empty-handed `@supports not (display: grid)` block that `tools/webos_ipk.py` requires); custom properties are fine (Chromium 49+) but not inside `@media` queries; and comments are scanned too, so do not spell a forbidden construct in a comment.
